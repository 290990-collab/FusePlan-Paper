\section{Experiments and Results}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}

% Sources (paper-comp/CVCS-AI-Assisted-ID/): damages src/vision/data/vision_damage.py,
% src/graph/graph_partial_query.py; self-recovery and AUC src/evaluation/robustness_auc.py;
% bootstrap src/evaluation/significance.py; removed area figures/historical/english/
% f_damage_kinds_valid.sources.txt (0.292/0.522/0.711); relevance src/evaluation/relevance.py;
% random floor PAPER.md §5.3 (test 0.7403/0.4663/0.8700); noise rule PAPER.md §5.8.

\paragraph{Incomplete queries.}
A query is a validation or test plan from which we remove a fraction $f\in\{0.25, 0.5, 0.75\}$ of the content with one of three damages, while the gallery keeps the complete plans.
\emph{Room removal} deletes $\operatorname{round}(f\,n)$ of the $n$ rooms, drawn with a per-query seed shared by the two branches, so both remove the same rooms.
In the image we fill the removed rooms with the background colour and erase their walls, so the query keeps only the outline of the hole; in the graph we keep the subgraph induced by the remaining rooms.
Rooms differ in size, so the removed area is on average 0.29, 0.52 and 0.71 of the plan at the three levels.
\emph{Crop} blanks one axis-aligned rectangle covering a fraction $f\pm0.05$ of the plan pixels, and \emph{patch} blanks whole ViT patches until they cover a fraction $f$ of the plan.
Crop and patch act on pixels, so only the vision branch receives them.

\paragraph{Robustness metric.}
We measure \emph{self-recovery}: the gallery still contains the intact source plan, and a query scores the reciprocal rank $\mathrm{rr}(q,f)$ of its source within the top 100, and 0 beyond.
The robustness of a system on a damage is the area under the self-recovery curve, $\mathrm{AUC}(q)=\tfrac{1}{3}\sum_{f}\mathrm{rr}(q,f)$, averaged over queries, as in the m@B metric of on-the-fly sketch retrieval~\cite{bhunia2020sketchless}.
Branches and fusions compete on room removal, the damage they share.
To select the vision configuration we average the AUC of the three damages, so that no configuration wins by exploiting one damage.
We exclude $f=0$: about 3\% of the RPLAN images have a pixel-identical twin, which caps the undamaged self-recovery near 0.97 for every system.
Self-recovery needs no relevance label, so the circularity discussed below does not affect it.

\paragraph{Statistics.}
We pair every comparison over the same queries, gallery and removed rooms, with 95\% percentile bootstrap intervals over queries ($B=10{,}000$).
Across four replicas, which change the graph training and the removed rooms, we call an effect \emph{above the training noise} when it has the same sign in 4/4 replicas and its mean exceeds twice the standard deviation across replicas.
For equivalence we use a margin of $\pm0.005$, fixed after the first replica and before the others.

\paragraph{Per-axis relevance (descriptive).}
To describe what a system retrieves on complete plans, we score its ranking with nDCG@10 on three relevance axes computed from the metadata, each in $[0,1]$.
\emph{Composition} is the weighted Jaccard similarity of the 13-bin room-type histograms; \emph{topology} is the weighted Jaccard similarity of the 91-bin histograms of adjacent type pairs; \emph{geometry} averages the similarities of footprint area, footprint aspect ratio and per-type area distribution.
A random ranking already scores 0.740, 0.466 and 0.870 on the three axes (test), so we also report the gain normalised to this floor, $(s-s_0)/(1-s_0)$.
These scores are circular for both branches and we never use them to select.
The graph receives the room types and adjacencies that composition and topology measure, and the footprint is the union of the room boxes in every record we checked.
The renderings encode the room types as colours, with 13 types collapsed into 6 colours.
Per-axis differences between the branches therefore compare a fine and a coarse reading of the same information.

\subsection{Dataset and experimental details}
\label{sec:setup}

% Sources: results/shared_gallery.json (67405 shared, 67453 images); PAPER.md §4, §5.1;
% history of test readings PAPER.md §7.6.

\paragraph{Dataset.}
RPLAN~\cite{wu2019rplan} provides, for each residential plan, a colour rendering ($1167\times875$ pixels, rooms coloured by category) and a record with 13 room types, room boxes and typed adjacencies.
The gallery contains the 67{,}405 plans that have both an image and a record (48 images lack a record) and both branches index it in the same order.
Following the official split, the gallery holds 47{,}126 training, 10{,}152 validation and 10{,}127 test plans.
Training and whitening statistics use training plans only, while the gallery always contains every plan, as in a search engine.
Graphs are small: 4 to 8 rooms, 6.8 on average.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
We make every choice (encoder configuration, checkpoint, fusion weight, control weights) on 2{,}000 validation queries drawn with a fixed seed, and evaluate on 2{,}000 test queries drawn in the same way.
Before each test reading we wrote the hypotheses, the predicted outcomes and the decision rules, then read the test split once with all weights fixed.
Earlier stages of the project also read the test split, with a weaker graph branch; those readings select nothing reported here, and we disclose them in \cref{sec:conclusion}.
Replicas use seeds 42, 100042, 200042 and 300042.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
\emph{hist} ranks plans by the L2 distance between room-type histograms, without training.
\emph{LayoutGKN}~\cite{vanengelenburg2025layoutgkn} is the closest published graph method; we retrain its GKN-asym network with its upstream hyperparameters on our pairs (damaged plan, complete plan) over the whole training split for 300 epochs, with edges from the record adjacencies, and score it with our protocol.
The same network with random weights reaches an AUC of 0.0001, so the retrained model learns the task.

\paragraph{A graph trained on incomplete plans comes close to the ceiling.}
\Cref{tab:main} and \cref{fig:damage_test} report the test results.
$W$ reaches an AUC of 0.903 and still ranks the source near the top with three quarters of the rooms removed (0.793).
Retrained on the same pairs, LayoutGKN scores 0.611, a paired difference of $-0.292$ $[-0.302, -0.282]$.
LayoutGKN stays close to $W$ when a quarter of the rooms is missing (0.940 vs 0.963) and drops when most are missing (0.217 vs 0.793).
The training-free histogram finds almost nothing (0.001), and the frozen vision branch reaches 0.396.
The standard deviation of $W$ across four replicas is 0.001 on the test split, so the graph result does not depend on one training.

\paragraph{Fusing with the frozen vision branch adds $+0.013$.}
The fusion with the frozen vision branch improves on $W$ by $+0.013$ $[+0.009, +0.017]$ on the test split, with a positive interval in all four replicas ($+0.0125\pm0.0012$) and a test value within 0.006 of validation in each.
Almost all of the gain comes at $f=0.75$ (0.832 vs 0.793), the only level where $W$ leaves room.
With the single-damage head the gain more than doubles, to $+0.031$ $[+0.026, +0.035]$, and with the multi-damage head the fused AUC reaches $0.940\pm0.001$.
The fusions also exceed a per-query oracle that picks the better branch for each query (0.914 for the frozen fusion), because summing similarities can rank the source above both branches on the same query.

\paragraph{With a strong graph, only a damage-trained vision branch adds information.}
\Cref{tab:controls} and \cref{fig:complementarity} compare each fusion gain with the ensemble controls.
Over four replicas, fusing $W$ with a second training of itself gains $+0.0163$ and fusing it with the SAGE graph gains $+0.0145$, both more than the frozen vision fusion ($+0.0125$).
Over four replicas the frozen vision gain is equivalent to the SAGE control ($-0.0020$ $[-0.0045, +0.0005]$, within $\pm0.005$) and slightly below the second copy of $W$ ($-0.0038$ $[-0.0067, -0.0010]$, negative in 4/4 replicas).
The single-damage head exceeds both controls by $+0.012$ $[+0.008, +0.015]$ and $+0.013$ $[+0.011, +0.016]$, and the multi-damage head by $+0.020$ $[+0.017, +0.023]$ and $+0.022$ $[+0.019, +0.025]$, all positive in 4/4 replicas and above the training noise.
The single-damage result was a pre-registered confirmatory test on seed 42 ($+0.014$ $[+0.008, +0.020]$ and $+0.015$ $[+0.011, +0.020]$) before the replicas.
Even when the complementarity gain is positive, the control accounts for 32--58\% of the fusion gain, so reporting the raw gain overstates the contribution of the image by about a factor of two.

\paragraph{The gain depends on the relative strength of the branches.}
\Cref{fig:curve} fuses the 37 graphs of the configuration search, from the weakest to $W$, with the two vision branches.
The gain follows an inverted U.
With graphs weaker than the vision branch it grows with the graph; it peaks when the branches are equally strong ($+0.198$ on test for a graph at 0.404 with the frozen vision branch); it shrinks as the graph approaches the ceiling ($+0.054$ at 0.753, $+0.013$ for $W$).
An earlier version of our pipeline used a graph at 0.456 on validation and measured a fusion gain of $+0.174$; that point lies on the same curve, so the large historical gain and the small gain with $W$ do not contradict each other.
The single-damage head lowers the gain with the weakest graph ($-0.019$ against the frozen branch), where it raises the reference branch, and raises it with medium and strong graphs ($+0.071$, $+0.066$, $+0.018$).
All nine pre-registered comparisons on the test split go in the predicted direction, in each of four seeds.
With graphs as strong as the vision branch, even the frozen vision branch passes both controls by $+0.08$ to $+0.16$ (\cref{tab:controls}, bottom).

\paragraph{The image helps where the graph has little left.}
\Cref{fig:where} locates the complementarity gain of the fusions with $W$ on the test split.
With a quarter or half of the rooms removed the CG is zero for all three vision branches, with intervals that contain zero; at $f=0.75$ it reaches $+0.046$ $[+0.031, +0.061]$ for the single-damage head and $+0.071$ $[+0.054, +0.087]$ for the multi-damage head.
On the 639 of 2{,}000 queries where $W$ misses the first rank at some level, the heads gain $+0.052$ and $+0.073$ over the SAGE control, while the frozen branch gains $+0.004$ $[-0.007, +0.016]$.
On the 154 plans with at most five rooms the CG grows to $+0.067$ and $+0.103$, and the frozen branch also exceeds zero ($+0.028$ $[+0.002, +0.053]$).
We expected a larger CG for plans that share their room graph with many others; the data refute it.
These groups overlap, since small plans and heavy damage both leave the graph with few rooms, and we apply no correction for the 39 group comparisons, so we read them as a description of one seed.

\paragraph{Takeaway.}
The vision branch contributes information of its own when the graph has little left: at heavy damage, on small plans, and with graphs no stronger than the image.
With a graph near the ceiling, a frozen image encoder adds no more than a second graph model, and the fusion gain alone would have suggested otherwise.

\paragraph{Graph branch.}
\Cref{tab:ablation_graph} shows that a temperature of 0.1 is the strongest single change for all three encoders ($+0.10$, $+0.17$ and $+0.23$ AUC over the reference), while $\tau=0.5$ and removing the skip from the input features hurt everywhere.

\paragraph{Vision branch.}
Whitening improves robustness in all 26 encoder--pooling pairs (\cref{tab:ablation_vision}a): without it the descriptors of the synthetic renderings collapse and damaged queries match almost any plan.
The choice of encoder matters more than pooling or resolution, and newer or larger encoders do not win: TIPSv2, the best encoder on complete plans in our benchmark, ranks sixth under damage, and the ViT-H I-JEPA trails two ViT-B models.
The best configuration on each single damage changes from damage to damage, which is why we select on their average.
The multi-damage head reaches a mean AUC of 0.715 on the three test damages against 0.521 for the frozen branch, and improves each damage with a positive interval (\cref{tab:ablation_vision}b).

\paragraph{Negative results.}
We report the outcomes that contradicted our expectations, because each one changed the protocol.
\emph{(i) A head learns the damage it sees.}
The single-damage head improves room removal by $+0.136$ on test and loses $-0.139$ on crop and $-0.101$ on patch, a pre-registered transfer hypothesis that we refuted on two encoders.
\emph{(ii) A rendering artifact inflated the vision robustness.}
Our first room-removal damage erased the dark perimeter walls and kept the lighter interior walls, so each removed room remained an empty outlined cell.
On the same queries and rooms, the artifact raised the AUC of a frozen DINOv3 configuration from 0.304 to 0.536 ($+0.336$ at $f=0.5$), and a head trained on that rendering exploited it.
We found the artifact because we had pre-registered a check on other damages; the three-damage criterion comes from this lesson.
\emph{(iii) The graph needs the right pairs and the right checkpoint.}
Trained on symmetric augmented pairs, every GNN ranks the source of a damaged query out of reach (AUC below 0.03) while recovering complete plans; asymmetric pairs fix this.
Selecting the checkpoint on complete plans picks early, erratic epochs: on one training trajectory it gives 0.107, against 0.457 when selecting on robustness.
For the graph, the InfoNCE validation loss even ranks configurations in the opposite order of retrieval, because in-batch negatives often share the composition of the anchor.
\emph{(iv) Refuted predictions.}
We predicted a SAGE configuration to win the search, the frozen fusion to gain more than $+0.12$ with $W$, and the frozen vision branch to be equivalent to the second copy of $W$; all three failed.
In the second round, 43 of 45 pre-registered predictions held, and the two failures concern this last point.

\subsubsection{Qualitative results}
\label{sec:qualitative}

% Sources: figures/final/english/f_final_teaser_valid.sources.txt (query 10071, 34 queries,
% head >100, W 2nd, fusion 1st); results/csv_final/test_summary.csv (full-plan nDCG@10).

\Cref{fig:teaser} shows a validation query with half of its rooms removed.
$W$ ranks first a near-identical plan and the source second; the single-damage head misses the source in its top 100; the fusion ranks the source first.
Its top-5 lists show the mechanism we observe across queries: one branch keeps the source near the top, and the other demotes the competitors that outrank it.
On complete plans the fusions change little.
On test, the frozen fusion scores 0.825, 0.662 and 0.953 nDCG@10 on composition, topology and geometry, against 0.818, 0.651 and 0.949 for $W$.
The fusion thus covers 33\%, 37\% and 64\% of the room above the random floor.
These per-axis numbers are circular and descriptive, and they show that complete-plan retrieval is far from solved even when self-recovery is near the ceiling.
