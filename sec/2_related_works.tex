\paragraph{Floor-plan datasets.}
RPLAN~\cite{wu2019rplan} provides more than 80{,}000 residential floor plans with room-level annotations, and is widely used for learning on floor plans~\cite{hu2020graph2plan,patil2021layoutgmn,vanengelenburg2025layoutgkn}.
Its layouts are comparatively simple, with mostly rectangular rooms, a limitation that motivates more recent datasets such as ResPlan~\cite{abouagour2025resplan}, which offers 17{,}000 vector plans with room connectivity; CubiCasa5K~\cite{kalervo2019cubicasa5k} instead targets the parsing of floor-plan images, with 5{,}000 plans densely annotated into more than 80 object categories.
We use RPLAN because, for the same plans, it provides both a rendered image and a structural record, the two inputs our branches compare.

\paragraph{Floor plans as graphs.}
Representing a plan as a graph of rooms connected by adjacencies is common in floor-plan generation.
Graph2Plan~\cite{hu2020graph2plan} generates a floor plan from a layout graph and a building boundary, and obtains its input graphs by retrieving, from RPLAN, plans that match the boundary and the user's room constraints.
Our graph branch uses the same kind of room graph, built directly from the RPLAN records, as the input of a retrieval encoder rather than of a generator.

\paragraph{Floor-plan retrieval and similarity.}
Retrieving floor plans requires a notion of similarity that is not purely visual.
LayoutGMN~\cite{patil2021layoutgmn} learns a structural similarity between layouts with an attention-based graph matching network trained on triplets whose labels come from pixel-wise IoU, and evaluates retrieval on RPLAN plans and user-interface designs.
LayoutGKN~\cite{vanengelenburg2025layoutgkn} postpones the cross-graph interactions to a differentiable graph kernel on the final node embeddings, which makes inference much faster than graph matching, and trains on RPLAN with a similarity based on graph edit distance, which the authors find to correlate with human judgement far better than IoU.
Both methods learn a similarity between complete plans from a pairwise target.
Neither addresses incomplete queries, which is the setting we study, and neither compares an image-based and a graph-based representation of the same plans or combines them.

\paragraph{Visual foundation encoders.}
Frozen general-purpose encoders are a natural baseline for retrieval on a new image domain.
We consider self-supervised encoders trained on curated data (DINOv2~\cite{oquab2024dinov2}, DINOv3~\cite{simeoni2025dinov3}), the joint-embedding predictive I-JEPA~\cite{assran2023ijepa}, which predicts the representation of masked target blocks from a context block and is therefore conceptually close to incomplete inputs, vision--language encoders (SigLIP~2~\cite{tschannen2025siglip2}, TIPSv2~\cite{cao2026tipsv2}), the agglomerative AM-RADIO~\cite{ranzinger2024amradio}, distilled from several teachers, and the Perception Encoder~\cite{bolya2025perception}, whose spatially aligned variant is tuned for dense features.
For global descriptors we follow standard practice in instance retrieval: generalised-mean pooling~\cite{radenovic2019gem}, PCA whitening of the descriptors~\cite{jegou2012whitening} and exact nearest-neighbour search with FAISS~\cite{douze2025faiss}.

\paragraph{Graph neural networks and contrastive learning.}
Graph encoders aggregate information from the neighbourhood of each node: GCN~\cite{kipf2017gcn} with a first-order approximation of spectral convolutions, GraphSAGE~\cite{hamilton2017graphsage} with learned aggregators that generalise inductively to unseen graphs, and GATv2~\cite{brody2022gatv2} with a dynamic attention that fixes the static attention of the original graph attention network.
We train the graph encoder with the InfoNCE objective~\cite{oord2019cpc}, contrasting positive pairs against in-batch negatives; our positives pair a partial plan with its complete version, so that the learned embedding targets the incomplete-query task directly.
